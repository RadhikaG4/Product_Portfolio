# PM Portfolio Case Study Structure: Frameworks, Length, Skimmability, Evidence

**Method note (read first):** The network proxy blocked full-page fetches for almost every domain tried (nngroup.com, uxtools.co, thecrit.co, uxplaybook.org, joinleland.com, medium.com subdomains). Findings below come from search-engine result summaries of those pages, not from reading them in full. Where the summary did not make clear which of several listed pages a claim came from, I say so ("attribution uncertain"). Nearly all of this is **practitioner opinion**. The only "data" is informal skim-time estimates from hiring managers, and none of it comes from controlled studies. Most sources are from 2023-2026. Laura Klein (2019) is older but still the standard PM-specific piece.

## 1. Recommended structures and frameworks (compared)

### Takeaway
Nearly every framework runs the same way: context, problem, approach/decisions, outcome, reflection. They differ mainly on two points: whether the result comes first (pyramid/TL;DR) and whether trade-offs and learnings get their own sections. PMs need a variant that treats **decisions** (evidence, options, trade-offs) as the core artifact, because "what you make most often is decisions." That means either a result-first pyramid or SPSIL with an explicit "options considered" block.

### Cited Findings
- **NN/g:** a 7-step process for a portfolio case study covering how you describe the design, the process that led to it, and its business impact. Case studies should include the problem or hypothesis, *your specific role*, and how you worked with others. A "common misconception" is that portfolios are only final UI and screenshots. — [NN/g, 5 Steps to Creating a UX-Design Portfolio](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-design-portfolios/); [NN/g video](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/ux-design-portfolio-case-study/)
- **PM-specific framing (Laura Klein):** designers and engineers have portfolios and GitHub, but "as a PM, what you make most often is decisions." She argues PMs should still build portfolios of their work. (2019, opinion) — [Users Know, Portfolios for Product Managers](https://www.usersknow.com/blog/2019/4/9/portfolios-for-product-managers)
- **Context–Problem–Solution–Wins** is described as a popular PM format. A longer variant: problem, user research, insights, solution, prioritisation, execution, metrics, results, lessons learned. — [search summary of PM guides incl. Leland, The Product Folks, HelloPM, CraftUp](https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/product-manager-portfolio) (attribution uncertain among these)
- **"Decision trail"** structure: problem → evidence → alternatives → decision → execution → metrics → learning. "Include the smallest set of artifacts that proves the decisions." Hiring signals named: discovery, funnel thinking, prioritisation, experimentation, measurement. — [CraftUp, PM Portfolio Projects](https://craftuplearn.com/blog/product-manager-portfolio-projects-get-hired) (attribution likely, via search summary)
- **Aakash Gupta:** arc of Problem → Research → Solution → Impact → Reflection, "with strong visuals and metrics for credibility." He also recommends proactive, company-specific case studies about the target company's product or problem area to get the portfolio opened. — [aakashg.com, PM Portfolio Examples That Win FAANG+ Offers](https://www.aakashg.com/product-manager-portfolio-examples/); [Aakash's newsletter](https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/product-manager-portfolio)
- **STAR** (Situation, Task, Action, Result) has been adapted to product design case studies. — [Carl Wheatley, Medium](https://carlwheatley.medium.com/structuring-your-product-design-case-studies-using-the-star-method-34eaae5c2de0); [Product School](https://productschool.com/blog/job-search/the-sar-method-for-product-management-interview-questions)
- **STAR critiqued for PMs:** IGotAnOffer argues STAR is "not the best" for PM work and proposes **SPSIL** (Situation, Problem, Solution, Impact, Lessons), which adds an explicit lessons step. — [IGotAnOffer, STAR method for PM interviews](https://igotanoffer.com/en/advice/star-method-product-manager-interview)
- **Amazon-style PR/FAQ** is mentioned as an alternative narrative frame for a PM case study. — [search summary, likely aakashg.com](https://www.aakashg.com/product-manager-portfolio-examples/) (attribution uncertain)
- **Minto Pyramid (answer first):** UX Playbook / Chris Nguyen recommend putting the conclusion/result first, then supporting arguments, then detail. — [UX Playbook, Minto pyramid guide](https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-case-study-minto-pyramid-structure-guide); [Chris Nguyen, Medium (2025)](https://uxchrisnguyen.medium.com/how-to-write-ux-case-studies-that-land-you-job-in-2025-00549454b39e)
- **Leslie Yang (Lyft design manager):** 3–4 case studies of "the work you want to be hired to do." Each should have short sections on user problem, business goals, research, iterations, visual design, and impact/metrics "if possible." She looks for initiative, product thinking, scope and innovation. — [Leslie Yang, Substack Part 1](https://leslieyang.substack.com/p/what-a-hiring-manager-looks-for-in); [Part 2 (senior signals)](https://leslieyang.substack.com/p/what-a-hiring-manager-looks-for-in-d25)
- **Sarah Doody:** a case study must say what you did, how you did it (process and thinking), and what happened (results/impact), and explain *why*, not only what. — [Career Strategy Lab, 5 steps](https://www.careerstrategylab.com/how-to-write-a-ux-case-study/)
- **Portfolio size:** 3–5 strong PM projects (launches, redesigns, feature improvements, teardowns, personal projects). For enterprise roles, favour complexity, scale and measurable value. — [search summary of PM guides](https://www.guvi.in/blog/product-manager-portfolio/) (attribution uncertain)

### Framework comparison (synthesised from the above)
| Framework | Order | Strength for PMs | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| STAR | Situation, Task, Action, Result | Familiar; matches behavioural interviews | No trade-off or learning step; "Action" hides decisions |
| SPSIL | Situation, Problem, Solution, Impact, Lessons | Adds reflection; PM-tailored | Still no explicit options/trade-offs |
| Problem–Process–Outcome (NN/g, Doody) | Problem, process, outcome | Simple, widely expected | Process can turn into a list of activities |
| Context–Problem–Solution–Wins | Context first | Short | Thin on evidence |
| Decision trail (CraftUp) | Problem, evidence, alternatives, decision, execution, metrics, learning | Best match for "PMs make decisions" | Longer; needs discipline |
| Minto / result-first | Result, then key arguments, then detail | Best for skimmers | Weak if there is no clear result |

### Inferences
- A strong PM template combines them: **TL;DR snapshot (result-first) → Context → Problem (with evidence) → Insight → Options and trade-offs → Decision/solution → Execution (brief) → Results (with baseline) → Learnings / what's next.** It is the decision trail with a Minto-style header.
- "Options/trade-offs" is the section that most separates PM case studies from design ones. Klein's point that decisions are the PM's output supports giving it its own heading.

### Gaps
- No source compared frameworks empirically (e.g., callback rates by structure). All comparisons are opinion.
- Could not read full-text Product School, Exponent or Lenny's pieces on PM *portfolio* structure. Lenny's content found concerned PM interviewing and hiring, not portfolios.

## 2. Length and layering for 30-second skimmers vs deep readers

### Takeaway
Hiring managers skim first. Informal estimates range from about 6 seconds for a first scan, to 30 seconds for a go/no-go decision, to 60–90 seconds per case study. Put the headline outcome and a short snapshot at the top, use scannable headings, and keep the body around 800–1,500 words with visuals (under about 10–12 minutes of reading).

### Cited Findings
- **Skim benchmarks (opinion/anecdote, not measured):**
  - "2 seconds and 30 seconds": 2s to decide whether to look further, then 30s more to decide whether to consider you. — [UX Tools, 5 Principles of Exceptional Case Studies](https://www.uxtools.co/blog/5-principles-of-exceptional-case-studies-in-ux-portfolios) (via search summary; attribution likely)
  - About 6–8 seconds on the initial portfolio scan for senior roles with 200+ applicants. The first 3 seconds go to the opening case-study title and first visual, then they skim for metrics and outcomes. — [UX University newsletter, "A hiring manager will spend 6 seconds on your portfolio"](https://newsletter.uxuniversity.io/p/a-hiring-manager-will-spend-6-seconds)
  - Average of 60 seconds per case study; about 90 seconds on the portfolio at first. — [search summary incl. UX Design Institute, NN/g video](https://www.uxdesigninstitute.com/blog/hiring-managers-ux-portfolio/) (attribution uncertain)
  - "Only 30 seconds to reject your portfolio." — [ADPList / AI First Designer Substack](https://adplist.substack.com/p/only-30-seconds-to-reject-your-portfolio)
  - **These figures conflict** (6s vs 30s vs 60–90s) because they measure different stages: initial scan vs per-case-study skim. None cites a study.
- **Reading-time decay:** recruiters read the first case study for 5–10 min, the second for 3–5, and the third for 1–2. Edit down anything over about 12 minutes. — [search summary incl. Aashrey Sharma, Career Strategy Lab](https://aashreysharma.com/how-long-should-your-ux-case-study-be) (attribution uncertain, opinion)
- **Word count:** 800–1,500 well-structured words plus visuals is "absolutely enough." Some advocate 800 words or fewer. "A 4,000-word case study that repeats itself... is weaker than an 800-word case study that makes every sentence count." — [Design Decade 2026](https://designdecade.org/how-to-write-a-ux-case-study-complete-structure-tips-and-examples-for-2026/); [Aashrey Sharma](https://aashreysharma.com/how-long-should-your-ux-case-study-be) (attribution between these uncertain)
- **Sarah Doody:** don't obsess over length. Recruiters want detail and process, but not "an essay or... a white paper." — [Career Strategy Lab, How long should a UX case study be](https://www.careerstrategylab.com/how-long-should-ux-case-study-be/)
- **Layering:** headline outcome, then a 30-second summary, then detailed sections. Put the headline result at the very top (e.g., "Cut checkout drop-off 23%"). — [TailorCV, How to Write a Portfolio Case Study](https://thetailorcv.com/blog/how-to-write-portfolio-case-study); [Open Doors Careers 2025](https://blog.opendoorscareers.com/p/how-to-write-a-strong-case-study-for-your-portfolio-in-2025-a14b) (attribution between these uncertain)
- **30-second recall test:** have someone scan the case study for 30s, close the tab, and ask what they remember. If they can name the problem, approach and outcome, it works. — [UX Tools / Open Doors Careers via search summary](https://www.uxtools.co/blog/5-principles-of-exceptional-case-studies-in-ux-portfolios) (attribution uncertain)
- Each section should carry "the essence" of your thinking so a skimmer gets the gist. Use headings, subheadings and bullets. — [UX Design Institute](https://www.uxdesigninstitute.com/blog/hiring-managers-ux-portfolio/); [search summary](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/ux-portfolios-hiring/)
- Make the case study easy to skim with clear headings, visuals and concise explanations, focusing on *why* decisions were made. — [search summary of PM guides](https://hellopm.co/product-manager-portfolio-guide/) (attribution uncertain)

### Inferences
- **Three-layer model for PMs:**
  1. **Title + hero line** (outcome-as-headline, about 3s).
  2. **Snapshot box** (about 30s): role, team, timeline, problem in one line, 2–3 key decisions, headline metrics with baseline, status (shipped / pilot / concept).
  3. **Body** (5–10 min): descriptive headings that state conclusions ("Onboarding, not pricing, drove churn" rather than "Research"), one annotated visual per section, and pull quotes for key user or stakeholder evidence.
- Put the best case study first, because attention decays across the second and third.
- Aim for about 1,000–1,500 words for a flagship PM case study. Supporting projects can be 400–800 words.

### Gaps
- No rigorous data (eye-tracking or A/B tests) on case study length or skim behaviour. All time figures are self-reported by hiring managers or authors.
- Found no source specifically on pull quotes. The recommendation above is an inference from general skimmability advice.

## 3. Presenting impact and metrics credibly

### Takeaway
Credible impact means **baseline → intervention → outcome** over a stated window, with one primary KPI plus two or three supporting signals, honest labels for estimated vs actual figures, and conservative wording about attribution. Put the headline metric at the top.

### Cited Findings
- A strong case study has a clear baseline with a stated comparison window (often 30–90 days), named source data (GA4, CRM, support logs), and one primary KPI (conversion, retention, expansion, revenue per user) supported by 2–3 UX signals. — [Lazarev.agency, UX ROI case studies](https://www.lazarev.agency/articles/ux-roi-case-studies) (agency marketing context, opinion)
- Evaluate case studies on "the measurable change they document: baseline, intervention, and outcome." — [Lazarev.agency](https://www.lazarev.agency/articles/ux-roi-case-studies)
- If you set no baseline, "estimate conservatively or use industry benchmarks." — [search summary, same result set](https://www.verlua.com/blog/website-case-study-examples) (attribution uncertain)
- In the first seconds, hiring managers skim "for metrics, outcomes, and evidence of strategic thinking." A first case study that doesn't signal impact loses them. — [UX University](https://newsletter.uxuniversity.io/p/a-hiring-manager-will-spend-6-seconds)
- Leslie Yang includes impact and metrics "if possible", so they are valued but not mandatory. — [Leslie Yang Substack](https://leslieyang.substack.com/p/what-a-hiring-manager-looks-for-in)
- For AI PM portfolios, a suggested evidence set is a behaviour spec, eval framework, failure analysis and metrics dashboard. — [Ishan, Building Your AI Portfolio as a PM](https://productishand03.substack.com/p/building-your-ai-portfolio-as-a-pm) (opinion, strong claim that this "will outperform" 10 years of PM experience is unsupported)

### Inferences
- Credibility checklist for PMs: (a) baseline value and date, (b) after value and window, (c) how it was measured (A/B test, pre/post, cohort), (d) your attribution ("the experiment arm showed…" vs "we contributed to…"), (e) label each figure *Actual*, *Estimated* or *Projected*, (f) include counter-metrics or guardrails you watched (e.g., support tickets did not rise).
- Pre/post comparisons without a control should say so. Seasonality and concurrent launches are the obvious confounders a sceptical PM interviewer will probe.
- For student or concept work, name leading indicators (usability task success, survey intent, pilot sign-ups) and do not dress them up as business outcomes.

### Gaps
- No PM-specific source found on labelling estimated vs actual figures or on attribution language. The recommendations above are inferences.
- No data on whether hiring managers discount unverified metrics.

## 4. Showing PM process artifacts without overwhelming

### Takeaway
Show the **smallest set of artifacts that proves the decisions**: one annotated visual per key decision, not a document dump. Relevant PM artifacts are problem framing, evidence (funnel, research synthesis), prioritisation (matrix or scoring), experiment design, and a spec excerpt.

### Cited Findings
- "Include the smallest set of artifacts that proves the decisions." Hiring signal: discovery, funnel thinking, prioritisation, experimentation, measurement. — [CraftUp](https://craftuplearn.com/blog/product-manager-portfolio-projects-get-hired) (via search summary)
- NN/g: portfolios are not only final screens. Show process and strategic thinking. — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-design-portfolios/)
- Leslie Yang wants "clear writing about user problems, business goals, research performed, and decisions made along the way." A portfolio is "a balance of show and tell." — [Leslie Yang Substack](https://leslieyang.substack.com/p/what-a-hiring-manager-looks-for-in)
- For candidates without work experience, rigorous research (market stats, competitive analysis, interviews) producing deliverables like empathy maps and personas builds credibility. — [search summary of CareerFoundry / PM guides](https://careerfoundry.com/en/blog/product-management/product-manager-portfolio/) (attribution uncertain)
- AI-PM portfolios: behaviour spec, eval framework, failure analysis, metrics dashboard. The case study "replaces long PRDs." — [Ishan Substack](https://productishand03.substack.com/p/building-your-ai-portfolio-as-a-pm)

### Inferences
- Map each artifact to the decision it supports: funnel chart → "where to focus", 2×2 or RICE table → "what we cut and why", experiment design card (hypothesis, metric, sample, duration, result) → "how we knew", PRD excerpt (problem statement + success metrics + non-goals) → "how I aligned the team", journey map or persona → "who and when the pain happens."
- Crop and annotate. Show a PRD's one-page summary, not all 12 pages. Link full artifacts as optional downloads for deep readers.
- Personas and journey maps are design-heavy and should only appear when they changed a decision.

### Gaps
- No source quantified how many artifacts is too many. No PM hiring-manager source on PRD excerpts specifically.

## 5. NDA and confidential work

### Takeaway
Anonymise (names, logos, colours, distinctive UI), use **relative numbers** (percentages, indexed values) instead of absolutes, abstract the visuals, and put the weight on process and decisions. Password protection is acceptable for genuinely sensitive work, but it creates friction that many recruiters resent. If you use it, put the password on the résumé and application, and keep some work public.

### Cited Findings
- Read the NDA thoroughly. Password-protected case studies shared only with prospective employers are fine "provided your client approves." — [LinkedIn Advice, Protect confidential UX projects](https://www.linkedin.com/advice/0/how-do-you-protect-confidential-ux-projects-skills-user-experience)
- Anonymise and write the case study around process, role and decisions, keeping details and final visuals behind a password. — [Ömer Arı, Medium](https://omerari.medium.com/how-to-add-nda-projects-to-your-ux-portfolio-without-breaking-trust-516730e42695)
- Replace logos, brand names and distinctive colour schemes. Swap product names for descriptive placeholders. Remove identifying data. "A stray logo in a corner, recognizable photography, or distinctive UI patterns can reveal client identity." — [Wonderlist, Showcasing Work Under NDA](https://www.wonderlist.design/insights/showcasing-work-under-nda); [IxDF](https://ixdf.org/literature/article/keep-it-confidential-how-to-showcase-your-nda-protected-design-work) (attribution between these uncertain)
- "Use percentages if you must talk about numerical metrics." — [IxDF, Handling NDAs in UX case studies](https://ixdf.org/literature/article/how-to-handle-non-disclosure-agreements-ndas-when-you-write-your-ux-case-study)
- Focus on the challenge, approach and outcomes without proprietary specifics, and on transferable insights. — [IxDF (Morgane Peng)](https://ixdf.org/literature/article/keep-it-confidential-how-to-showcase-your-nda-protected-design-work)
- Other NDA guides: [UX Planet, Tamila Zamotailo](https://uxplanet.org/nda-how-to-show-confidential-work-in-your-portfolio-ea165058bb96); [vanschneider, DESK](https://vanschneider.com/blog/portfolio-tips/make-portfolio-work-cant-shared/); [Good Designer Substack](https://gooddesigner.substack.com/p/presenting-confidential-design-work)
- **Password cons:** password protection is "a conscious choice to add friction." Recruiters complain about passwords not being included with résumés, which forces back-and-forth. One poster claims "in most cases, there's no way the password-protected work is actually NDA material." — [Blind: "Password Protected Portfolios are driving me crazy"](https://www.teamblind.com/post/password-protected-portfolios-are-driving-me-crazy-jlg5y1gp); [Blind: "is pw protection driving recruiters away"](https://www.teamblind.com/post/is-pw-protection-driving-recruiters-away-mep7yi3h) (anonymous forum, anecdotal)
- **Hybrid approach:** keep some public work for visibility and credibility, plus a private section for NDA or tailored case studies. — [Designfolio, "I Reviewed 500+ Portfolios"](https://designfolio.substack.com/p/portfolio-tips-ux) (attribution likely)
- Leslie Yang: include the portfolio link **and password** on both the application and the résumé. — [Leslie Yang Substack](https://leslieyang.substack.com/p/what-a-hiring-manager-looks-for-in)
- It's reasonable to share the password with an employer. Change it regularly and track who has access. — [UX Playbook FAQ](https://uxplaybook.org/faq/how-to-add-nda-projects-to-portfolio)

### Inferences
- For PMs: redraw the funnel with indexed values (baseline = 100) or percent changes. Describe the company generically ("Series C B2B payments platform, ~X employees"). Replace screens with simplified wireframe-style diagrams. Keep the decision logic intact, because that is what's being evaluated and it's rarely the confidential part.
- A public version with redacted numbers plus a password-gated "full numbers" version, with the password on the CV, balances friction against confidentiality.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on how much password gating reduces views or callbacks.

## 6. Student, live/consulting, side projects vs full-time work; individual contribution

### Takeaway
Non-employment projects are legitimate evidence if you label them honestly (type, client, duration, team) and treat them rigorously: real users, explicit constraints, a learning loop. For team work, state your role precisely, name the other roles, and use "I" for your decisions and "we" for team outcomes.

### Cited Findings
- Student projects and side hustles can show what you can do when employment work can't be shared. "You don't need real-world experience to build credibility." — [search summary of CareerFoundry / PM guides](https://careerfoundry.com/en/blog/product-management/product-manager-portfolio/) (attribution uncertain)
- If you lack professional PM experience, side projects fill the gap and should cover problem, approach, decisions and outcomes. — [ProductPeople, Building a Portfolio](https://productpeople.co/guides/building-portfolio)
- "A project that shows a clean learning loop can be more credible than one that makes you look perfect." Add "one evidence-led project with reachable users and explicit constraints." — [CraftUp](https://craftuplearn.com/blog/product-manager-portfolio-projects-get-hired) (via search summary)
- Proactive teardown or redesign case studies about a target company's product can win interviews. — [aakashg.com](https://www.aakashg.com/product-manager-portfolio-examples/)
- **Role clarity:** strong portfolios label the candidate's specific function (e.g., "I led visual design while a UX researcher handled user interviews and a copywriter produced content"). Weak ones present team outcomes without boundaries. When role is missing, hiring managers ask in interviews and may suspect the candidate hoped they wouldn't. — [The Crit, Portfolio Project Examples](https://www.thecrit.co/resources/portfolio-project-examples); [iCreatives, 7-Step Portfolio Review](https://www.icreatives.com/iblog/the-7-step-portfolio-review-process-every-creative-hiring-manager-needs/) (attribution between these uncertain)
- Note role, responsibilities, who else was on the team, and project duration. Acknowledge others' contributions. — [IxDF, How to Write Great Case Studies](https://ixdf.org/literature/article/how-to-write-great-case-studies-for-your-ux-design-portfolio)
- Each case study should show "your particular contribution to achieving a necessary business goal." — [search summary incl. Users Know / TailorCV](https://www.usersknow.com/blog/2019/4/9/portfolios-for-product-managers) (attribution uncertain)
- NN/g: state your specific role and how you collaborated. — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-design-portfolios/)

### Inferences
- Add a project-type tag in each snapshot: *Full-time*, *Internship*, *Live client/consulting project*, *Academic (course/competition)*, *Side project*, *Speculative teardown*. Labelling honestly avoids the credibility hit of being caught overstating.
- Rank live/consulting and full-time projects above academic ones. Case competitions can be framed like consulting engagements, with a brief, constraints, recommendation and judge outcome.
- Add a "My contribution" line or table (owned / co-owned / supported) to separate personal decisions from team execution.

### Gaps
- No data on how hiring managers weight student vs professional projects.

## 7. Unfinished outcomes, failures, "what I'd do next"

### Takeaway
Failures and unfinished work are acceptable, and can raise credibility, when framed as a learning loop. Say what you noticed, how you tested whether it was a dead end, why you stopped or pivoted, what you left behind, and the sharper question the work answered. End with concrete next steps.

### Cited Findings
- A good failed-project write-up covers noticing, testing whether it's really a dead end, deciding to stop, and "leaving something useful behind." Because the original goal may have been the wrong question, "end with the sharper question your work actually answered rather than what you didn't deliver." — [Apurva Bhure Substack, "How do you write a case study about a project that didn't work?"](https://apurvabhure.substack.com/p/how-do-you-write-a-case-study-about)
- A clean learning loop can be more credible than looking perfect. — [CraftUp](https://craftuplearn.com/blog/product-manager-portfolio-projects-get-hired)
- SPSIL and Aakash's arc both end in Lessons/Reflection. — [IGotAnOffer](https://igotanoffer.com/en/advice/star-method-product-manager-interview); [aakashg.com](https://www.aakashg.com/product-manager-portfolio-examples/)

### Inferences
- For unshipped or in-progress work: state status plainly in the snapshot ("Pilot, 6 weeks of data", "Recommendation accepted, build Q3", "Concept, not built"). Show the leading indicators you have and the success criteria you'd use.
- The "What I'd do next" section works best as 2–4 specific moves (next experiment, metric to instrument, segment to test). It shows forward product judgement.
- Keep reflection short (3–5 bullets) and specific ("I'd have set the baseline before launch") rather than generic.

### Gaps
- Only one practitioner source addressed failed-project case studies directly. No hiring-manager data on how failure narratives are received.
